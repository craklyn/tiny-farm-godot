# Phase 5 — The Wilds

*Stub — deliberately the thinnest. Genre itself is deferred (D-1); pre-production starts
only when phase-4 bots demonstrably fight with learned behaviors, because the bots ARE the
party.*

**Premise:** With the farm self-running (the phase 4→5 proof), the player ventures out to
strike the pests (or another enemy — D-3) at the source, leading an X-COM-like party of
themself plus their trained bots. Maybe tactical, maybe a "weird team Gradius 3-like"
hybrid. TBD on purpose.

**Design intent (what we do know):**
- The farm persists as home base; expeditions are excursion maps (P-3).
- Navigation into the wilds follows the enemy's own infrastructure (P-10): pest trails
  are trackable backward to their nest. The road to the final act is written in scent by
  the thing you're hunting — and bots with the smell sensor can read it.
- Squad members are *your* bots with *your* trained models — expedition performance
  reflects how you raised them. Losses, if any exist, should matter accordingly.
- The interface question (tactical tap-command vs. twitch) is the flagship decision made
  here, with P-1's adjustment clause on the table.
- Emergent possibility, zero committed scope (ruled 2026-08-18): comms interception —
  enemies with audio perception can hear bot pings, and vice versa. Falls out of channel
  symmetry (P-7); decide whether to use it at D-1.

**Open questions (all of them — at D-1):**
- Genre: turn-based tactics, real-time-with-pause, autobattler-with-orders, or twitch
  hybrid?
- Do expeditions feed back into farming (resources, new crops, captured pests as
  training data)?
- Ending: is there a final source-of-the-pests boss, and what does "done" mean for the
  whole game?
