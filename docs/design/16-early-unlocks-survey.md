# Early unlocks without a purchase

Date: 2026-09-24 · Status: LIVING · Last updated: 2026-09-24 · Repo/location: `docs/design/16-early-unlocks-survey.md`

## Background and scope

This repairs the survey on HQ card `wfce637ce465`. Its first answer omitted colony sims, compressed three other genres into one sentence, and lived only on the card. The live card itself ends Daniel's quotation at **“Note that if w”** and its `first_action` at **“given: the player may not be able to read ”**. Those missing endings are TK; this note does not reconstruct them. The ranking uses the known pre-reader constraint in `13-teaching-and-onboarding.md` and remains provisional until the missing requirements are recovered.

This is a comparison of delivery patterns, not a seed-economy or robot-unlock implementation. The current prototype already gives three plantable units per harvest, carries ten per species, and reserves the first ten per species in the shipping bin (S-18–S-20, `docs/DECISION_LOG.md`; `docs/design/02-farming-system.md`). These numbers are marked `[Playtest]`. A grain silo's future forty-unit rule is settled, but the silo is not playable; its acquisition method is not settled (`docs/SEED_ECONOMY_PLAN.md`). Q-55 parks pea-ammo and tower logistics for later design (`docs/DESIGNER_QUEUE.md`).

## Method

Compare seven delivery patterns named in the card on three questions: what action earns the unlock, what the player learns from seeing it, and what Tiny Farm must build beyond its present code and art. “Low / medium / high” below is a relative design estimate, not measured engineering time. External examples are tied to developer or publisher descriptions where possible. A genre comparison tests which part of each pattern transfers; it does not assume every mechanic in another game belongs here.

## Pattern findings

| Pattern and sourced example | Lesson to player | Relative build cost for Tiny Farm and main risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Timed shipment or gift.** *Oxygen Not Included* uses care packages in its Printing Pod; [Klei's update notes](https://forums.kleientertainment.com/forums/topic/165991-game-update-the-prehistoric-planet-pack-dlc-beta-671500/) show that seeds can enter that pool. | A new possibility can arrive as the world advances. | **Medium:** day scheduling exists, but a visible arrival, eligibility table, save/replay state, and missed-gift handling need design. A random package must never be the only route to starter planting. |
| **Help someone, receive something.** *Stardew Valley* [Community Center bundles](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Bundles) exchange gathered sets for rewards; this official game wiki is community maintained, so treat it as mechanics documentation rather than a developer statement. | Work for another person can change the shared world. | **High:** a character need, request display, acceptance, completion, and reward all need a picture-based vocabulary and tests for no-reading play. The existing neighbour lesson does not yet constitute that system. |
| **Find a tool or ability in the world.** Nintendo describes *Metroid Dread*'s [discoverable upgrades](https://metroid.nintendo.com/dread/samus/) and the doors, blocks, and routes they open. | Exploration pays off; a visible obstacle becomes newly passable. | **Medium:** pickup is simple, but fair placement, obstacle preview, navigation after acquisition, and save/replay proof are substantial. This fits Tiny Farm's fenced land rings better than an unexplained inventory drop. |
| **Milestone proof.** Wube describes [Factorio's action-triggered technologies](https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-376): mining or crafting can reveal a capability without lab research. *Hades* [stages early weapon visibility](https://www.supergiantgames.com/blog/hades-the-nighty-night-update-patch-notes/) behind earlier unlocks and returning from an attempt. | “What I just did caused this.” | **Low to medium** if an existing action/proof can be reused; **higher** if a new counter or UI is needed. A proof must be legible and non-grindy. Tiny Farm already has phase proofs, but the robot proof presently opens a **priced** shop card (`12-progression-and-gates.md`), so “milestone” here would need a separate grant rule. |
| **Make the next thing from owned materials.** Mojang's [starter guide](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/how-minecraft) walks from collected wood to planks, a bench, and tools. | A familiar material has a second use. | **Low** for the already implemented crop-replant loop; **medium or high** for a new recipe UI, ingredients, quantities, and edge cases. A crop-to-ammo conversion would be new Q-55 work, not part of this survey. |
| **Repair something visible.** The initially broken [Stardew greenhouse](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Greenhouse) is restored through bundles (official community wiki). | The landscape shows an attainable future, and repair changes it in place. | **Medium to high:** damaged/finished art, material or proof rules, action feedback, placement, and migration. A broken grain silo is an *option*, not an approved feature or a cost-free way to add the forty-unit capacity. |
| **Season or weather opening.** *Stardew* [seasonal crops](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Crops) give the calendar mechanical meaning (official community wiki). | Watch the world for a recurring opportunity. | **High** for seasons because crop, art, balance, and pest rules multiply; Tiny Farm has explicitly deferred seasons (P-11, `02-farming-system.md`). **Medium** for a weather-only event using existing rain, but random weather must have a fallback if it grants an essential tool. |

## Genre comparison and transfer

- **Farming/life sims:** Stardew bundles and its broken greenhouse join contribution, gift, and repair into one visible chain. That makes restoration emotionally clear, but the bundle ledger and delayed payoff are too much for Tiny Farm's first seed lesson. Its seasons also create opportunities that this project's current scope has declined.
- **Survival crafting:** Minecraft begins with a material already in reach and turns it into tools. The player can repeat the chain without a timer or shop. Tiny Farm's current harvest-to-replant loop has this causal strength, but a new crafting screen would add a separate learning burden. Reuse the direct plantable unit first.
- **Colony sims:** *RimWorld*'s [developer-authored description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/294100/RimWorld/) names research specialists, storyteller events, traveling salesmen, and incoming refugees. These are **distinct gates**: work accumulated by the colony can unlock capabilities; arrivals can offer new people or goods, but a trader is still a money route and a random arrival is unreliable. *Oxygen Not Included* adds Printing Pod care packages, showing a timed arrival that can expand resources. The colony lesson for Tiny Farm is to separate a deterministic **proof of sustained production** from a **delivery spectacle**. If future storage is earned by excess harvest, the stock threshold needs a recoverable, per-species rule and a visible reason; a day-based gift can present the reward, but should not decide whether the player can keep farming. Factorio's action-triggered technology is a useful adjacent factory-sim example of proof by doing, while its science-pack research tree would be too much infrastructure for a pre-reader's early unlock.
- **Roguelikes:** *Hades* offers [chamber reward previews](https://www.supergiantgames.com/blog/hades-welcome-to-hell-update-patch-notes/) and grants region-clear bounties, while its [weapon sequence](https://www.supergiantgames.com/blog/hades-the-nighty-night-update-patch-notes/) makes some permanent options visible only after prior ones. The useful transfer is **small, immediate, visually previewed rewards after a proved action**, with a choice only after the basic loop is understood. A run reset or random reward pool would undermine a guaranteed starter-seed route; a permanent unlock also needs a save/replay contract that a temporary boon does not.
- **Metroidvanias:** *Metroid Dread* makes the new ability useful against a previously seen physical obstacle: Morph Ball fits narrow passages; beams and missiles affect specific covers and blocks ([Nintendo ability guide](https://metroid.nintendo.com/dread/samus/)). This is more precise than “beat boss, gain item.” For Tiny Farm, show the blocked parcel and distinct debris before granting a tool, then visibly open that same route. The cost lies in map layout and feedback, not only in the pickup. It is strongest for land/tool progression, weaker for abstract surplus accounting.
- **Tower defence:** EA says *Plants vs. Zombies 2* players [earn seed packets by play](https://www.ea.com/games/plants-vs-zombies/plants-vs-zombies-2/features), use them for stronger plants and abilities, and advance through levels and scheduled quests. That combines performance rewards with a content ladder; a new defence option is understandable when the next threat gives it a job. For Tiny Farm, a future pea-using tower should appear when pests create a readable need and after pea supply is reliable. Wave-clear rewards need a fallback in this farm game: early crop replenishment cannot depend on winning a defensive wave. A new tower also costs targeting, placement, ammo logistics, art, and wave balance, not just an unlock flag.

## Provisional selection for this game

1. **Owned-material reuse:** retain harvest-to-replant as the early supply lesson. It is already implemented and guaranteed by player action; test whether a pre-reader sees the plantable unit as both crop and next planting. Do not infer a pea-ammo interface from it.
2. **Visible milestone proof:** let a demonstrated action open the next capability, using the existing gate vocabulary. Candidate: repeated successful production could announce that future storage is available. The proof and whether it grants or merely offers a silo are still design questions; the current robot proofs open priced cards and should not be silently rewritten.
3. **Visible repair/restoration:** a derelict silo on acquired land could give a physical target for the storage step. This ranks behind the first two because it requires new world art, resource rules, persistence, and validation. It is a design candidate, not a ruling that silo acquisition is by repair.

The ranking favors reliable, player-caused, visually testable access over chance and text. It may change if the missing end of Daniel's quotation or ranking constraints specifies a different audience, timing, or acquisition rule. Those two endings are **TK**; the card has no fuller text to resolve them. The survey itself can be used now for a separate decision or strawman, but neither the research note nor its ranking authorizes implementation.
