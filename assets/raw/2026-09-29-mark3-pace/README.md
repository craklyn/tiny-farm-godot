the Mark III leaning into motion with two short trails (S-34, from Daniel's Q-131

## What each call settled

- **The three text-prompted poses** (first three rows) are lively but draw a
  different robot: a teal dome head with a side visor, where the farm's Mark III
  has a pale grey face with two amber eyes. Cut down to the farm robot's size
  they go dark on the shelf's dark card. The most legible of the six,
  `pace_profile_run_0`, is candidate C. The model drew speed streaks and a ground
  shadow under most of them even though the prompt said not to; the rebuild
  keeps only the largest connected shape.
- **The image-to-image lean** did not lean: one image kept the standing pose,
  the other redrew the head. Neither contributed pixels.
- **The run cycle** is the useful result. Given the shipped sprite, the
  animation model returned eight frames of the same robot that stay on the
  input's 2×2 pixel grid (99% of blocks uniform), so halving it recovers the
  farm sprite's own pixels with no redrawing. Candidates A and B come from it.

## How the candidates are made

`build_pace_candidates.py` rebuilds everything in `docs/design/mockups/pace_picture/`
from these raws and the real shelf capture there (`0_today_full.png`, taken with
`tools/capture_workbench_shelf.tscn`):

- Every candidate is snapped to `bot_mk3.png`'s own colours and drawn at the farm
  sprite's density, so on the shelf it sits at 3×, the same scale as the
  starting-brain card's robot below it. The robot is 29 pixels tall there, so the
  picture is 40×30 (120×90 on screen), 18 pixels taller than today's 120×72 box;
  the card is 104 pixels tall and has the room.
- **A (still)** is frame 4 of the run cycle, sheared forward six pixels at the
  top. **B (moving)** is all eight frames with the same shear, looping at 150 ms a
  frame; A is B's frame 4, so they differ only in motion. **C (new drawing)** is
  `pace_profile_run_0` reduced by majority vote to the same 28-pixel height.
- The two trails are drawn locally, never generated, and are identical on all
  three: one-pixel lines in the shelf's ink at the 72% it uses today, upper one
  seven pixels and lower one five, ending two pixels short of the robot.
